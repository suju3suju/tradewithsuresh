Market Structure 101: HH, HL, LH and LL

Learn to read trends using market structure — higher highs, higher lows, lower highs and lower lows — the foundation of price action trading.

Before indicators, before strategies, there is market structure — the simple language the chart uses to tell you whether buyers or sellers are in control.

The four building blocks

Price never moves in a straight line. It moves in waves of pushes and pullbacks, leaving behind a series of swing highs and swing lows. Reading the sequence of those swings is market structure.

  • Higher High (HH): a peak that is higher than the previous peak.
  • Higher Low (HL): a dip that is higher than the previous dip.
  • Lower High (LH): a peak that is lower than the previous peak.
  • Lower Low (LL): a dip that is lower than the previous dip.

Reading the trend

When you see a run of higher highs and higher lows, buyers are in control — that is an uptrend. When you see lower highs and lower lows, sellers are in control — a downtrend. When highs and lows are roughly flat and overlapping, the market is ranging, with no clear winner.

Why it matters

Most trading mistakes come from fighting the structure — buying in a clear downtrend or shorting a strong uptrend. Once you can label the swings, you naturally trade with the dominant side instead of against it.

Spotting a change of trend

A trend often shifts when structure breaks. For example, in an uptrend of HH and HL, the first time price makes a lower low and fails to make a new high, it is an early warning that buyers may be losing control. These structure breaks are some of the cleanest signals price action offers.

Putting it together

  • Mark the recent swing highs and swing lows on any chart.
  • Label each as HH, HL, LH or LL.
  • Read the sequence to decide: uptrend, downtrend or range.
  • Combine with support and resistance zones for higher-quality decisions.
